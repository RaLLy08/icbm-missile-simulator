Physics Units and Models

This simulation was built following the International System of Units (SI). Below are the units, constants and equations the Ballistic Missile Launch simulator uses to model the missile and the Earth.

SI base units

The flight model needs only three of the seven SI base units. Every other quantity is derived from them.

Quantity Unit Symbol Used for
Length metre m Missile position, altitude, Earth radius
Mass kilogram kg Payload (warhead) mass, fuel mass of each stage, total missile mass, Earth mass
Time second s Simulation time step, flight time, fuel burn time, gravity turn duration

SI derived units

Quantity Unit In base units Used for
Velocity metre per second m/s Missile velocity vector, engine exhaust velocity
Acceleration metre per second squared m/s² Gravitational acceleration, acceleration produced by the engine
Force newton N = kg·m/s² Engine thrust, \(F = \dot{m}\, v_e\)
Mass flow rate kilogram per second kg/s How fast each stage burns its propellant \((\dot{m})\)
Plane angle radian rad Thrust pitch angle during the gravity turn
Angular velocity radian per second rad/s How fast the thrust vector tilts towards the target \((\omega)\)
Gravitational constant — m³/(kg·s²) Newton's law of universal gravitation

Missile

A missile is a core object representing the movement of the missile through space, based on its physical parameters described below. See the source code: Missile.ts.

Accepted parameters

Input values that define the missile. They are set before launch and stay fixed during the flight.

Parameter Description Unit
Earth The Earth the missile flies around. It gives the gravity and altitude at the missile's position (see Earth). —
Launch position Point on the Earth's surface where the missile starts. m
Payload mass Mass of the warhead or cargo. It is what remains after all fuel is burnt. kg
Fuel mass Mass of propellant a stage carries at ignition. kg
Exhaust velocity Speed at which the engine expels exhaust gas. A higher value gives more thrust from the same amount of fuel. m/s
Mass flow rate How much fuel the engine burns every second. kg/s
Gravity turn start altitude Altitude after which the thrust starts to tilt from vertical towards the target. m
Gravity turn duration Maximum time the thrust keeps tilting. s
Gravity turn angular velocity How fast the thrust tilts towards the target. rad/s

Derived parameters

Values calculated from the accepted parameters at every simulation time step.

Parameter Description Calculation Unit
Total mass Payload mass plus the fuel not yet burnt. \(\text{payload mass} + (\text{fuel mass} - \text{burnt fuel})\) kg
Fuel Combustion Time Time until the fuel is burnt. \(\displaystyle\sum_\text{stages} \frac{\text{fuel mass}}{\text{mass flow rate}}\) s
Thrust (as acceleration) Acceleration from the engine. It grows as fuel burns off and the missile gets lighter. Based on the rocket thrust equation. \(\dfrac{\text{mass flow rate} \times \text{exhaust velocity}}{\text{total mass}}\) m/s²
Thrust angle Tilt of the thrust from vertical towards the target. \(\text{angular velocity} \times \text{time since the turn started}\) (up to the gravity turn duration) rad
Velocity Speed and direction of the missile. \(\text{velocity} + (\text{gravity} + \text{thrust}) \times \text{time step}\) m/s
Position Location of the missile. \(\text{position} + \text{velocity} \times \text{time step}\) m
Altitude Height above the Earth's surface. \(\text{distance to the Earth's centre} - \text{Earth radius}\) m
Flight time Time since launch. \(\text{flight time} + \text{time step}\) s

Multi-stage missile

A multi-stage missile drives a missile through several engine stages. The missile carries the fuel of all stages at launch. Stages burn in order: when the burning stage runs out of fuel, the next one ignites, and the missile's exhaust velocity and mass flow rate switch to that stage's values. If a time step crosses a stage boundary, it is split there, so each stage burns exactly its own fuel. See the source code: MultiStageMissile.ts.

Accepted parameters

Set before launch. Stages with no fuel or a zero mass flow rate are skipped.

Parameter Description Unit
Missile The missile that the stages power (see Missile). —
Stages Ordered list of engine stages. Each stage has the three values below. —
Stage fuel mass Mass of propellant in this stage. kg
Stage exhaust velocity Exhaust velocity of this stage's engine. m/s
Stage mass flow rate How much fuel this stage burns every second. kg/s

Derived parameters

Calculated from the stages and passed on to the missile.

Parameter Description Calculation Unit
Total fuel mass Fuel of all stages, set as the missile's fuel mass. \(\displaystyle\sum_\text{stages} \text{fuel mass}\) kg
Fuel combustion time Time until the last stage burns out. \(\displaystyle\sum_\text{stages} \frac{\text{fuel mass}}{\text{mass flow rate}}\) s
Current stage The stage burning now: the first stage whose fuel is not yet used up. first stage where \(\text{burnt fuel} < \displaystyle\sum_\text{up to this stage} \text{fuel mass}\) —
Stage remaining fuel Fuel left in the burning stage. \(\displaystyle\sum_\text{up to current stage} \text{fuel mass} - \text{burnt fuel}\) kg
Stage time left Time until the burning stage burns out. \(\dfrac{\text{stage remaining fuel}}{\text{stage mass flow rate}}\) s

Earth

The Earth is a sphere at the centre of the scene. Its gravity is the only external force acting on the missile. See the source code: Earth.ts.

Equations and how the units combine

Gravity

Gravity is not a fixed 9.81 m/s². At every time step it is computed from Newton's law of universal gravitation and points to the Earth's centre:

\[ g = \frac{G M}{r^2} \qquad \left[\frac{\mathrm{m^3}}{\mathrm{kg\,s^2}} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m^2}} = \mathrm{\frac{m}{s^2}}\right] \]

Here \(r\) is the distance from the Earth's centre in metres. At the surface this gives about 9.80 m/s².

Thrust

Each engine stage is described by its exhaust velocity \(v_e\) (m/s) and mass flow rate \(\dot{m}\) (kg/s). Their product is the thrust force (see NASA: Rocket Thrust Equation):

\[ F = \dot{m}\, v_e \qquad \left[\mathrm{\frac{kg}{s} \cdot \frac{m}{s} = \frac{kg\,m}{s^2} = N}\right] \]

The simulator divides the force by the current mass of the missile (payload plus remaining fuel), which gives the acceleration from the engine:

\[ a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{\dot{m}\, v_e}{m_\text{payload} + m_\text{fuel}} \qquad \left[\mathrm{\frac{N}{kg} = \frac{m}{s^2}}\right] \]

Burn time

\[ t_\text{burn} = \sum_\text{stages} \frac{m_\text{fuel}}{\dot{m}} \qquad \left[\mathrm{\frac{kg}{kg/s} = s}\right] \]

For a multi-stage missile the total burn time is the sum over all stages.

Gravity turn

The missile launches straight up, against gravity. Once it passes a set altitude (in m), the thrust starts to tilt towards the target at a constant angular velocity \(\omega\) (rad/s) for a set time (in s):

\[ \theta = \omega\, t \qquad \left[\mathrm{\frac{rad}{s} \cdot s = rad}\right] \]

Motion

On each time step \(\Delta t\) (in seconds), the total acceleration updates the velocity, and the velocity updates the position:

\[ \begin{aligned} \vec{v} &\leftarrow \vec{v} + (\vec{g} + \vec{a})\,\Delta t && \left[\mathrm{\tfrac{m}{s} + \tfrac{m}{s^2} \cdot s}\right] \\ \vec{x} &\leftarrow \vec{x} + \vec{v}\,\Delta t && \left[\mathrm{m + \tfrac{m}{s} \cdot s}\right] \end{aligned} \]

Not considered

  • The Earth is a perfect sphere with radius 6,378 km and does not rotate.
  • There is no air drag or lift.
  • Exhaust velocity and mass flow rate are constant within each stage. Thrust does not change with altitude.
  • The empty mass of spent stages is not modelled. Only payload and fuel count towards the mass.
